## 🏔️ Lake Tahoe Basin: Winter vs. Summer Intelligence

Lake Tahoe requires two completely different operational mindsets depending on the season:

### ❄️ Winter Strategy (November – April)
- **Snow & Access:** Snowpack typically ranges from 4 to 15+ feet at lake level (6,225 ft) and up to 25 feet on passes (Donner Summit 7,200 ft, Carson Pass 8,574 ft). Caltrans **R2 chain controls** (chains required on all vehicles except 4WD/AWD with snow-tread tires on all 4 wheels) are standard during storms on I-80, US-50, and Hwy 89.
- **Official Snow Camping:** **Sugar Pine Point State Park (West Shore / Tahoma)** is the premier state park in the entire Tahoe Basin that remains open for official vehicle winter snow camping. Camp loops are plowed, restrooms are heated with hot running water, and 12+ miles of marked Nordic ski and snowshoe trails start right at your campsite.
- **Sno-Parks:** **Blackwood Canyon Sno-Park** (Kaspian area, North Shore) and **Donner Summit Sno-Park** provide plowed staging areas for winter backcountry camping, ski touring, and snowmobiling. Requires a California Sno-Park permit ($15/day or $40/season, available online or at local gear shops).
- **Ski Area Proximity:** North Lake campgrounds (Donner Memorial, William Kent, Kaspian) are 10–20 minutes from **Palisades Tahoe, Alpine Meadows, Sugar Bowl, and Northstar**.

### ☀️ Summer Strategy (June – September)
- **Beaches & Water:** Water temperature peaks at 65–70°F in August. **Meeks Bay** offers Caribbean-turquoise water with soft white sugar sand. **Sand Harbor** features granite monolith coves. **William Kent** and **Sugar Pine Point** provide easy beach access under Jeffrey pines.
- **Hiking & Backpacking:** The **Tahoe Rim Trail (TRT)** encircles the lake (165 miles), with immediate access into **Desolation Wilderness** via Meeks Creek, Eagle Falls, and Echo Lakes.

## 📊 Multi-Field Rating System Explained

To avoid inflating scores where every park lands above 9.0, each destination is evaluated across four distinct 10-point dimensions:
1. **🥾 Hiking & Trail Access (1–10):** Direct trailhead access to premier singletrack, elevation changes, alpine wilderness connectivity, and waterfalls.
2. **🌅 Scenery & Views (1–10):** Natural grandeur, coastal drops, dramatic granite monoliths, mountain reflections, and dark skies.
3. **⛺ Comfort & Amenities (1–10):** Quality of restrooms (heated/flush vs vault), hot showers, potable water, site spacing, shade, and RV hookups.
4. **⚡ Booking Competition (1–10):** Practical difficulty of reserving a spot (10 = sells out in seconds on drop day, e.g. Steep Ravine, Upper Pines; 2–4 = walk-up FCFS or plentiful dispersed camping).
